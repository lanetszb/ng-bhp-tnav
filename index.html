Gas rel perm exponent ng as a function of BHP in tNavigator

Question: can the modified Brooks-Corey gas curve krg = 1 - ((Sw - 0.1)/0.9)^ng use an exponent that depends on the flowing bottom hole pressure, ng = 2.1065 - 0.1726 Log(Pwf)?

tNavigator has the Corey and LET correlations built in, but no keyword makes an exponent a function of pressure. What it can do is change the saturation table number (SATNUM) of any cell during the run. So the curve is tabulated for a ladder of ng values, one table each, and a small Python script attached with APPLYSCRIPT reads each well's BHP every time step, computes ng from the formula and assigns the nearest table to the cells around that well. Both cases below were run this way on a small dual-porosity CBM test model: 15 x 15 x 2 cells, three wells, tNavigator 26.2, CPU and GPU give identical results. Log is taken as the natural log and Pwf in psi; both are assumptions to be confirmed.

Case 1. 24-month drawdown, then BHP held on a limit

Panel A shows the curve itself at five values of Pwf. As BHP falls, ng rises from 0.91 to 1.43 and the curve lifts, so gas becomes more mobile at the same water saturation. The dashed line is a conventional Corey-type table for comparison: at the low gas saturations where a dewatering coal operates it sits far below the formula curves, which is why the choice of curve form changes gas far more than ng does.

Panel B compares three runs. Gray lines have ng frozen at the two ends of the formula's range: light gray at the 70 bar value, dark gray at the 5 bar value. Blue is ng following the well BHP through the script. During the drawdown the blue line starts on the light gray curve and crosses to the dark gray one; the rates differ from either fixed case by up to about 30 percent in that period.

Panel C shows why the blue line then merges with the dark gray one: BHP reaches 5 bar at month 24 and stays there, ng stops changing, and for the remaining two years the model behaves exactly like a fixed ng at the limit value. The 4-year cumulative ends within one percent of the high-ng run.

Case 2. BHP managed continuously, never on a limit

Same model and script, but BHP is lowered a little every month for all four years, so ng is never constant. Now the blue line keeps its own path: it stays near the low-ng curve while BHP is high, so at two years the cumulative is 23 percent below the high-ng run, then it migrates across and ends the life with the highest rate of the three, because gas held back early is released late. The 4-year cumulative is 31.3 t against 25.8 t and 32.4 t for the two fixed bounds.

What this means in practice: the BHP dependence changes the result only while BHP is moving. Wells parked on a BHP limit are indifferent to it, and a fixed ng at the limit value gives nearly the same answer. Wells under continuous drawdown management, staged drawdown or pump speed changes keep ng moving, and then the dependence shapes both the timing of gas and the total. On a field model it is a short test to quantify once the formula details are confirmed: log base and pressure unit, near-well zone or whole grid, per well or field BHP.

Deck lines that make it work, in SCHEDULE before WELSPECS: ARITHMETIC_ARRAYS SATNUM / and APPLYSCRIPT 'ng_bhp.py' 'ng_bhp' 6* / 2.1065 0.1726 'LN' 14.5038 '[1.4, 1.3, 1.2, 1.1, 1.0, 0.9]' 2 / /. Arguments: A, B, log base, bar to psi factor, the ng ladder in table order, box half-width in cells. Decks, script and README available on request.
